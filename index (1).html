About This Place

An instrument built to read the cosmos

Maharaja Jai Singh II of Jaipur erected Jantar Mantar in New Delhi (then Shahjahanabad) in 1724 as part of a network of five astronomical observatories across India. Its massive masonry instruments—dials, quadrants, and sundials—allowed astronomers to track the sun's declination, determine local time, and predict eclipses with remarkable accuracy.

Notable Movements

Voices that changed India

2011

India Against Corruption

Anna Hazare's fast catalysed India's largest anti-corruption movement, demanding a strong Lokpal institution. The protests drew unprecedented middle-class participation.

The movement, anchored at Jantar Mantar and later Ramlila Maidan, saw celebrities, students, and professionals join hands. It put corruption at the centre of national political discourse and ultimately led to the passage of the Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act in 2013.

2020–21

Farmers' Rights Movement

Hundreds of thousands of farmers from Punjab, Haryana, and Uttar Pradesh marched to Delhi's borders to protest three controversial farm laws.

After over a year of sustained protest, the Indian government repealed the three Farm Acts in November 2021—a historic victory demonstrating the power of organised, peaceful civic resistance and one of the longest-running protests in Indian democratic history.

2019

CAA/NRC Protests

Students, women's groups, and civil society organisations gathered at Jantar Mantar to protest the Citizenship Amendment Act, raising questions about constitutional equality.

Protests against the CAA spread across India's cities and campuses. Jantar Mantar served as a rallying point where protesters held peaceful sit-ins, read the Preamble to the Constitution aloud, and engaged in dialogue about secularism, citizenship, and the democratic fabric of the nation.

Pillars of Democracy

What Jantar Mantar represents

Freedom of Expression

Article 19 of the Indian Constitution guarantees every citizen the right to freedom of speech and peaceful assembly—rights exercised visibly at Jantar Mantar.

Right to Peaceful Assembly

Citizens have the constitutionally protected right to assemble peacefully without arms. Jantar Mantar is a living example of this right in action.

Accountability of Power

Its proximity to Parliament makes Jantar Mantar uniquely powerful—a direct line between citizen grievances and legislative authority.
